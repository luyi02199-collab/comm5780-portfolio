A LITTLE ABOUT ME

About Me

I'm Stella Yang, a postgraduate Digital Media student at the University of Leeds. I am interested in digital culture, online communication and creative media.

This portfolio records my learning in COMM5780M. Through weekly reflections and practical work, I explore how digital methods shape what we can learn about people, platforms and everyday life.

WEEKLY LEARNING

Workshop Reflections

This section is being updated with reflections on the weekly workshops, including my methods, findings, challenges and ideas for improvement.

01 / WEEK 1

Week 1: Introducing Digital Practices

This week introduced different ways of understanding the relationship between technology and society. I found the social shaping approach useful because it considers how technology influences society while also being shaped by people, institutions and social contexts.

The discussion of generative AI helped me think beyond whether a technology is simply helpful or harmful. Its effects on learning depend on how it is designed, used and governed. This encouraged me to ask who makes decisions about digital tools and whose interests they serve.

Throughout this module, I want to develop practical skills while also questioning the assumptions and limitations of the tools I use.

02 / WEEK 2

Week 2: Creating and Hosting a Website

In this workshop, I developed my portfolio using HTML, CSS and GitHub Pages. I personalised the homepage with an introduction, colours and a cover illustration.

One challenge was getting the cover image to display correctly. This helped me understand the importance of file names and paths, and of checking the published website rather than only the local version.

Looking back, I initially focused more on appearance than content. I now recognise that a portfolio should demonstrate my learning through practical work and reflection. Going forward, I will update it regularly and make the workshop reflections easy to find.

03 / WEEK 3

Week 3: Web Scraping

For this task, I used the Web Scraper Chrome extension to extract programme titles from BBC iPlayer and exported the results as a CSV file.

Selecting the correct webpage elements was difficult. My first attempts returned no text. Inspecting the HTML helped me identify a selector that extracted programme titles.

However, the titles were combined in one cell, and some appeared more than once. This showed me that extracting data does not automatically make it ready for analysis. Next time, I would improve the structure and check for duplicates before analysing it.

05 / WEEK 5

Week 5: Data Visualisation

In this workshop, I used the ESS UK dataset provided by the teaching team to create a pie chart about voting behaviour in WPS Spreadsheets.

I learned that making a chart requires more than selecting data and choosing a design. The numerical codes represented categories, but my pivot table added them together instead of counting responses. This meant the chart did not accurately show the proportions of respondents.

This experience showed me how software settings and decisions about categories can shape the message of a visualisation. Next time, I would check the codebook and calculation method, and use clear labels so readers can understand the chart.
